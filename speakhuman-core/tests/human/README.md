# Human corpus

Writing by people, from before chat models, used by `run_tests.py` to check that the linter does not call ordinary human writing slop, and by `tools/rule_evidence.py` to record what each rule costs on it. The test lints every file here with house style off (dashes and Title Case are preferences) and fails when the whole corpus passes 0.6 errors or 5.5 warnings per 1,000 words, when any genre passes its own ceiling in `GENRE_CEILINGS`, or when more than 3 of the 60 commit messages carry an error. Warnings count too, because the author has to read each one before keeping or changing it.

The genre ceilings are today's measurement plus a margin, so a rule change that fires on one kind of writing shows up even when the corpus as a whole stays low. They record where SpeakHuman stands today. The table below is generated by `tools/rule_evidence.py --write` with the test's settings, and the suite fails when it is out of date:

Blog posts are the genre the skills target most, and they run above the whole-corpus error ceiling: the 18F authors write `empowered` (four times, in `empowered product owner`), `exactly` for emphasis (four times), `leverage a` and `you are not alone`. 69 of the 74 commit-message warnings are `markdown-leak` on one team's `**Why**:` template. A rule change that pushes a genre over its ceiling is firing on normal human writing: make that rule a warning or narrow it. Closers such as `Let me know if you have any questions` were six of the eight errors in a sample of business email from 2000 and 2001 when they counted as chatbot residue. People wrote them long before chatbots, so they are now the house-style rule `stock-email-courtesy`, which a profile can switch off.

## Business email (kept out of this repository)

The maintainer also runs the suite on 212 emails from the sent folders of 74 Enron employees (the Enron email dataset, released by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission in 2003 and distributed by Carnegie Mellon University). They are real people's correspondence, so they stay out of this repository and out of every zip, even with email addresses and phone numbers removed. To run the business-email genre yourself, save a JSON list of message texts as `enron-sent-mail.json` in this folder: the suite picks it up, holds it to its own ceiling, and leaves it out of the generated table above. The file is listed in `.gitignore`.
